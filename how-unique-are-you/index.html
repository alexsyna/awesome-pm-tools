Why this matters

Removing names isn't enough

In 1997 a researcher found the Massachusetts governor's hospital record in "anonymous" health data using only his ZIP code, birth date and sex. A few ordinary details are often enough to single someone out.

Blurring is the fix

Anonymization makes details less precise (ages in ranges, country instead of city) until everyone shares their combination with several others. That's the "k" in k-anonymity; 5 is a common minimum.

A crowd isn't always enough

If all 5 people in your crowd earn the same salary band, anyone who knows you're in that crowd knows your salary. Good anonymization also checks that private answers vary inside each group.
